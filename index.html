01 — My Story

The Heart of
My Innovation

"Braddy! Ven la comida ya está." — "Wait one second Mami." Three hours later, I had finally completed my invention: the Spiderman web shooter. I immediately grabbed some rubber bands to attach the mini tube to my arm, extended my hand, and the pen shot out hitting the wall with a satisfying thump.

My mother has always been supportive of my creativity, whether it was making a cardboard robot or creating my own math problems at age 7. When I proudly showed my family the web shooter — "Wow! Qué bueno" — I felt alive. That euphoric feeling of a challenge conquered has driven every project since.

From debugging microcontrollers after school to mastering a basketball eurostep through sheer repetition, I've learned that persistence and creative thinking are the same muscle. I aspire to apply my innovation to the frontier of AI.

Age 11 · Mechanical Engineering

RubberBand Helicopter

One of my very first inventions — built purely out of curiosity and a love for Spiderman. I constructed it using a G-2 Pilot pen with a string attached to the barrel to create a shooter mechanism. It worked like a catapult: a spring held the pen and the cap served as a release button. When fired, I could pull the pen back with the string — just like a web. The moment it worked was the spark that started everything.

High School · Hardware + Sensors

Compost Encourager

Students often skip composting because it's tedious and confusing. Our device uses a capacitive sensor to measure the electromagnetic signature of items placed in front of it. If the reading falls within the compost threshold, it cheers "Good job!"; otherwise "Try again." Instant, intuitive feedback makes sorting feel almost like a game. We presented at the National Grid competition and other major events.

Senior Year · Web Development

Compost Encouraging Website

Built during senior year to market the Compost Encourager. Using Replit, HTML, CSS, and JavaScript I created a web presence that communicated the device's value to judges and the public. I integrated an API and interactive elements to demonstrate how it works live. The site was central to our National Grid competition presentation.

School Project · Microcontrollers

Microchip Pinball Game

Inspired by pinball, this two-player physical game was built using a microcontroller programmed through a dedicated development environment. Each player holds a controller — pressing its button rotates a servo arm exactly 90 degrees. A wooden ball sits center-board, and both players try to kick it into the opponent's goal. First to 10 wins. My first project bridging software with physical, real-world gameplay.

Cornell Makeathon · Arduino + OLED + RFID

Watch Buddies

Built with a team at the Cornell Makers Club Makeathon, Watch Buddies is a wearable companion device designed to give people a little friend by their side throughout the day. The watch houses an Arduino Nano, a 128×64 OLED display, an RFID sensor, and a battery — all inside a custom 3D-printed enclosure with a watch strap that I helped CAD.

My contributions spanned both hardware and software. On the hardware side, I designed the housing to snugly fit the Arduino Nano, OLED screen, battery pack, and RFID reader. On the software side, I helped code the RFID sensor logic and worked on the stick-figure animations that play across the OLED display — 15-frame walking cycles and reactive poses stored as bitmap arrays in PROGMEM. When you scan your RFID tag, your buddy reacts with a greeting like "Good morning!" and plays an animation.

The goal was simple but meaningful: make people feel a little less alone during their day.
